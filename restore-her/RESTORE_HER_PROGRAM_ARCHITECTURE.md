# Restore Her — Program Architecture
### Lake Street CDC | Strategic Execution Plan, Manual, and Operating System

Prepared for Lori Porter. This document turns the Restore Her concept into an executable, train-the-trainer program while protecting the kitchen-table spirit of the original model.

**Working assumptions (labeled where made):**
- *Assumption:* "Bi-weekly" means every two weeks (not twice a week). Weeks 1–9 = roughly 4–5 sessions per group before the retreat.
- *Assumption:* Group A (Executive Track) and Group B (Organic Track) meet as separate 12-woman circles during Weeks 1–9, and come together for the first time as a full 24-woman body at the Week 10 retreat. This preserves intimacy in the weekly sessions while still building the cross-group relationships needed for Month 11 pairing.
- *Assumption:* Lori is the lead facilitator for both groups initially; a co-facilitator may be needed by Week 5 if bandwidth becomes a constraint (flagged as a risk below).
- *Assumption:* The retreat venue is a residential house/retreat property with a real kitchen — not a hotel or conference center.
- *Assumption:* The org already operates on Google Workspace (Docs, Sheets, Drive, Gmail, Meet).
- *Assumption:* Budget for the workforce/re-entry tracker favors a Google Sheets or Airtable build in Phase 1, not a custom database.

Where an assumption materially changes the plan if wrong, it's flagged again in context.

## A. Restore Her Strategic Execution Plan

### Phases

| Phase | Timing | What Happens | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — Pre-Launch | Now → Week 0 (next 30 days) | Finalize manual, naming, venue, registration, recruit both groups | Lori + Admin |
| 1 — Curriculum | Weeks 1–9 | Bi-weekly sessions, both groups, in parallel | Lori |
| 2 — Retreat | Week 10, Fri noon–Sun | Full 24 together, stress-test manual, begin pairing prep | Lori + Admin |
| 3 — Selection & Pairing | Month 11 | Rubric scoring, duo pairing, facilitation prep | Admin + Lori |
| 4 — Graduation & Induction | Month 12 | Formal induction into Restore Her Sisterhood, leaders commissioned | Admin |
| 5 — Duplication (ongoing) | Month 13+ | Graduate duos launch their own circles; workforce/re-entry pipeline scales alongside | New Leaders + Admin |

### Priorities (in order)

1. Lock the naming decision (Section F) — everything else (manual, registration, folders) references this name, so deciding late means rework everywhere.
2. Finalize the 12-page manual v1 (Section B) — Lori needs it before Week 1, and it's the artifact future leaders will actually use.
3. Confirm the Week 10 retreat venue and dates — home-style venues with 24-person capacity book out; this is the highest lead-time item.
4. Stand up registration with the hard commitment checkbox (Section E) — must go out before anyone is confirmed into either group.
5. Recruit and confirm all 24 women (12 + 12).
6. Build the leadership rubric and workforce tracker in parallel during Weeks 1–9 (not urgent for Week 1, but needed before Month 11 and before any re-entry placements begin).

### Dependencies

- Naming decision → registration copy, manual title page, folder structure. **Blocks everything downstream if undecided.**
- Manual v1 → Week 1 session (Lori is running from it starting day one).
- Retreat venue booked → registration copy (participants need real dates/location to agree to).
- Leadership rubric → Month 11 pairing (can't pair without scores).
- Workforce/re-entry tracker → any re-entry placement work; not a Week 1 blocker, but should exist before Month 12 graduation so graduates can be routed immediately.

### Decision Points (need a yes/no from Lori/Admin, not assumptions)

| Decision | Why it matters | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Does Lori facilitate both groups solo through Week 9, or is a co-facilitator brought in? | Affects Lori's capacity and consistency of tone across groups | Start solo; pre-identify a backup co-facilitator by Week 5 in case of burnout or scheduling conflict |
| Is the retreat at a private home or a rented residential property? | Capacity for 24 + overnight logistics may exceed a single home | Rented residential retreat house with a full kitchen — same warmth, more capacity |
| Are duos self-selected (with input) or fully admin-assigned? | Affects buy-in vs. objectivity of pairing | Admin-assigned using the rubric + retreat observation, with participants submitting a short "who energizes me" preference sheet as one input — final call stays with Admin |
| Does the workforce/re-entry track launch alongside Weeks 1–9, or wait until graduation? | Early launch builds employer relationships sooner but adds operational load during curriculum | Begin employer relationship-building (e.g., Ashley's cleaning company) in Phase 1 quietly, but don't onboard participants into placement tracking until Month 11–12 |

### Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Attrition over 9 bi-weekly sessions | Registration checkbox sets expectations up front (Section E); Lori does a personal check-in call if someone misses two sessions |
| Week 10 retreat causes late dropouts if surprised by it | Retreat date and agreement is presented and signed at registration, not discovered later |
| Scaling flattens the kitchen-table intimacy | Hard cap groups at 12; manual explicitly bans commercial venues (Section B, Page 2); every future circle must be home-based |
| Lori becomes the single point of failure | Co-facilitator identified early; manual is written so someone other than Lori could run a session from it |
| Re-entry data is sensitive (criminal history, placement status) | Restrict the workforce tracker to Admin + case manager access only; no participant names in shared/general folders (Section I) |
| Pairing feels arbitrary or political | Rubric (Section G) + documented rationale for every pairing, shared privately with the pair, not the whole group |

## B. 12-Page Manual Architecture

Hard constraint: 12 pages, no more. Each page is plug-and-play — a future leader with no other training should be able to run a circle from this alone.

| Page | Title | Objective | Key Content | Leader Can Now... |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover — What Restore Her Is | Set the tone before anyone reads a word | One-paragraph origin + mission, the promise ("this is a table, not a program") | Explain Restore Her in one breath to a new participant |
| 2 | The Non-Negotiables | Protect the model from drift | Home/kitchen-table setting only, no commercial venues, group size cap of 12, confidentiality rule, what Restore Her is *not* (not therapy, not church, not a class) | Say no to pressure to move the model into an office or classroom |
| 3 | What a Session Feels Like | Paint the room before explaining the mechanics | Sensory description: food on the table, seating in a circle, phones away, tone of voice | Set up a room that *feels* right even with no other instructions |
| 4 | The Leader's Role | Define the job | Before: prep food/space; During: hold time, hold silence, don't dominate; After: follow-up texts/calls | Know exactly what to do before, during, and after |
| 5 | The Meeting Framework (Cheat Sheet) | One page they can prop against a cookbook mid-session | Arrival → Opening → Discussion → Reflection → Accountability → Closing, with approximate minute counts | Run a full session start to finish without notes |
| 6 | The Nine-Week Curriculum Map | Show the arc | One line per session: theme + one guiding question (e.g., Week 1: Identity, Week 3: Wounds & Walls, Week 7: Voice) | See where any given week sits in the whole journey |
| 7 | Holding Space, Not Fixing | Core facilitation skill | Difference between holding space and problem-solving; how to redirect advice-giving; how to sit in silence | Facilitate without taking over the room |
| 8 | When It Gets Hard | Handle the moments the curriculum doesn't script | What to do with tears, disclosure of abuse/crisis, conflict between two women, someone shutting down | Respond calmly instead of freezing or over-managing |
| 9 | Materials & Setup Checklist | Remove all guesswork | Physical checklist: food, seating, candle/centerpiece, tissue, name of the week's theme written somewhere visible, phone basket | Set up a session in 15 minutes with zero improvisation |
| 10 | The Retreat & What Comes After | Preview the arc beyond Week 9 | What Week 10 retreat is, why it's mandatory, what pairing/graduation mean | Explain to a participant why the retreat isn't optional |
| 11 | Launching Your Own Circle | The duplication instructions | Minimum standards to start a circle (home setting, 8–12 women, use of this manual, check-in cadence with Lake Street CDC), what *not* to change | Start their own circle within the guardrails |
| 12 | The Sisterhood Covenant | Close with commitment, not just information | Short covenant text, signature line, contact info for Lake Street CDC support | Feel formally commissioned, not just informed |

## C. Meeting Framework (Reusable Session Structure)

Designed for a 90-minute bi-weekly session around a kitchen table.

| Segment | Time | What Happens | Leader Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrival Rhythm | 15 min | Women arrive to food already out, low music, no formal start — leader greets each woman by name at the door | Never start "on the dot." The arrival *is* part of the session, not dead time before it. |
| Opening | 10 min | Leader lights a candle or equivalent ritual cue, states the week's theme in one sentence, opens with a grounding question ("What's one word for how you walked in tonight?") | Same ritual cue every week — repetition builds safety. |
| Discussion Flow | 35 min | Theme-based prompt from the curriculum map, leader shares first (models vulnerability), then opens the circle — no cross-talk, no advice-giving, one voice at a time | Leader should speak *second-to-last* naturally by circling back, not dominate the middle. |
| Reflection Questions | 15 min | 2–3 standing questions repeated weekly with theme-specific variation: *What did I hear tonight that I needed? What am I still carrying? What's one thing I'll do differently before we meet again?* | Keep the same three question *stems* all nine weeks — it builds a rhythm participants can lean on. |
| Accountability | 10 min | Each woman states one small commitment for the next two weeks out loud to the group | Leader keeps a simple private log (Section H structure can double for this) to follow up between sessions. |
| Closing | 5 min | Circle stands, hands joined or a closing phrase said together, leader names one strength she saw in the room that night | Closing must feel ceremonial, not administrative — no "ok see you next time" energy. |
| Leader Follow-Up (post-session) | Within 48 hrs | Personal text/call to any woman who was quiet, upset, or absent | This is where retention actually happens — not in the room. |

## D. Week 10 Retreat Agenda (Friday Noon → Sunday)

Purpose: stress-test the manual, bring both groups together for the first time as one body, and begin preparing all 24 for leadership.

**Friday**
- 12:00 PM — Arrival, lunch (cooked together, not catered — this is the tone-setter)
- 1:30 PM — Opening circle: full 24 together for the first time, Lori frames the weekend's purpose
- 2:30 PM — Group teach-back: small mixed teams (mixing A and B) each run a 20-minute mock session using the manual, everyone else observes and gives written feedback
- 5:30 PM — Break / free time
- 6:30 PM — Dinner cooked together
- 8:00 PM — Evening reflection circle (low-key, no agenda beyond honesty)

**Saturday**
- 8:00 AM — Breakfast
- 9:00 AM — Leadership traits session: rubric introduced (Section G), self-assessment + peer feedback
- 11:00 AM — Small-group discussion: what did this program change in you?
- 12:30 PM — Lunch
- 1:30 PM — Pairing process: Admin presents the pairing approach; women complete a short preference sheet (not a vote — one input among several)
- 3:00 PM — Practice facilitation: each woman leads a 10-minute segment solo, timed feedback from Lori + one peer
- 5:30 PM — Free time / rest
- 6:30 PM — Dinner
- 8:00 PM — Covenant rehearsal: walk through the graduation covenant language (Section E) so it isn't a surprise in Month 12

**Sunday**
- 8:00 AM — Breakfast
- 9:00 AM — Final practicum debrief: what worked, what the manual is missing (this feedback becomes manual v2)
- 10:30 AM — Standards for launching a circle (manual page 11) walked through explicitly, Q&A
- 11:30 AM — Closing commissioning circle: each woman named individually, one sentence of affirmation
- 12:30 PM — Lunch and departure by 2:00 PM

## E. Registration and Onboarding Copy

**Participant Commitment Language**

> Restore Her isn't a class you attend — it's a table you sit at. For the next twelve months, you're committing to show up for yourself and for the 11 other women sitting with you. That means every session, every two weeks, for nine weeks. It means one mandatory weekend retreat. It means being honest even when it's easier not to be. If you're ready for that, we're ready for you.

**Retreat Agreement Language**

> On [Week 10 date], Restore Her holds a mandatory working retreat, beginning Friday at 12:00 noon and running through Sunday afternoon. This is not optional and it is not rescheduled around individual conflicts — the whole point is that all 24 of us are in the room together. Please confirm this date works before you register. If something changes later, tell us as early as possible so we can problem-solve together, but plan on being there from the start.

**Checkbox Confirmation Language (registration form — all required)**

- [ ] I understand Restore Her meets every two weeks for nine weeks, and I'm committing to attend every session.
- [ ] I understand the Week 10 retreat is mandatory, runs Friday 12:00 PM through Sunday afternoon, and I confirm this date works for me.
- [ ] I understand Restore Her is preparing me for leadership, not just support — I'm saying yes to growing into that.
- [ ] I have read and agree to the participant commitment above.

*Registration cannot be finalized until all four boxes are checked.*

## G. Leadership Selection Rubric

Score each woman 1–5 on each trait during Weeks 1–9 (ongoing) and finalize at the retreat. Used for Month 11 pairing, not for exclusion — everyone graduates; this determines pairing and readiness sequencing.

| Trait | 1 (Not Yet) | 3 (Developing) | 5 (Ready) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relational Maturity | Reacts defensively to feedback | Receives feedback with some friction | Receives feedback openly, adjusts without defensiveness |
| Consistency | Frequently absent/late | Mostly present, occasional lapses | Fully present, reliable every session |
| Emotional Safety | Shares others' stories outside the room | Generally discreet | Actively protects confidentiality, others feel safe around her |
| Accountability | Deflects or blames when she falls short | Owns it eventually, sometimes with prompting | Owns mistakes immediately, follows through on commitments |
| Communication | Dominates or withdraws | Participates, sometimes uneven listening/speaking balance | Listens fully, speaks with care, makes room for others |
| Hospitality | Focused inward | Warm to those she already knows | Makes anyone — even a stranger — feel at home |
| Fidelity to the Model | Wants to redesign the format around her own style | Follows the format with minor personal flourishes | Runs the model as-is, resists over-personalizing it |

**Pairing logic:** each duo should have complementary strengths (e.g., one strong in hospitality + consistency paired with one strong in communication + fidelity to model), not two women with the same gaps.
